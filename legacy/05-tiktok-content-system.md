# 05 — TIKTOK CONTENT SYSTEM

The content engine. The single biggest lever in the entire business.

---

## 1. The three-account network

Three accounts feed each other. Each has a distinct role.

### @[brandname] — the main account

**Role:** the artifact channel. Pure object content.
**Format:** dossier reels, 15–22 seconds.
**Frequency:** 2 posts/day, every day.
**Goal:** drive profile clicks → bio link → store.

### @[brandname].field — the world account

**Role:** the world channel. Behind-the-scenes of the fiction.
**Format:** longer ambient clips, 45–90 seconds.
**Examples:**
- Hands sorting through papers in a dim room
- Magnifying glass on a fictional document
- Watch being wound on a desk by candlelight
- Wax seal being broken
- Ambient field recording (rain, city at night, tape recorder hiss)
**Frequency:** 3–4 posts/week.
**Goal:** deepen the world. No products sold here.

### @[brandname].notes — the literary account

**Role:** the text channel.
**Format:** static black background, single sentence in serif type. Sometimes a faded photograph. Sometimes a fictional letter fragment.
**Frequency:** 4–5 posts/week.
**Goal:** generate saves and shares (text posts get insane save rates).

**Why three accounts win:**
- 3x the algorithmic surface area
- Cross-pollination: the field/notes accounts make the main feel like the tip of an iceberg
- Customers feel "in the know" by following all three
- Competitors literally cannot copy this without 6+ months of operational lift

## 2. The four content pillars (main account)

Every dossier reel is one of four types. Rotate evenly.

### Pillar 1 — The Reveal (40% of posts)

The standard dossier reel. New product, full mythology, soft close. **This is the conversion driver.**

### Pillar 2 — The Detail (25%)

12–15 second extreme macro of ONE detail of one watch. The seconds-hand sweep. The crown knurl rotating. Light catching on a beveled lug.

**Crucially: the full product is never shown.** Caption is one cryptic line:
> *"You can hear the rotor if you hold it to your ear."*

These drive profile clicks because the brain wants to see the whole object.

### Pillar 3 — The Frame (20%)

The watch in an environment. Zero people in frame. The implied protagonist is the buyer's mental self-image.

Examples:
- On a hotel desk next to a half-empty glass
- Beside a closed notebook on raw linen
- On the dashboard of an old car at night
- On a stack of books on a windowsill
- On an unmade bed beside a paperback

This is where "I want to BE the person who owns this" lives.

### Pillar 4 — The Found Document (15%)

A single photograph (or short pan) of a fictional document — a faded receipt, a torn map, a typed letter — with the watch visible somewhere in the frame.

Caption: one line of mythology.

These are cheap to produce (AI-generated docs + watch macro) and get high engagement.

## 3. The dossier reel anatomy

Every Reveal post follows this structure exactly.

```
0–1.5s   HOOK
         Macro detail (no full watch yet)
         On-screen text: LOT 0049 / GENEVA / 1991
         Audio: silence or single tactile sound (paper rustle, click)

1.5–4s   DISORIENTATION
         Cut to unrelated-looking detail
         Examples: a torn paper, a typed signature,
                   a polaroid, a wax seal fragment
         Brain begins pattern-completion = retention spike

4–10s    REVEAL
         Full object placed on surface or rotated
         Voiceover, ONE sentence:
         "Belonged to a man who never gave his real name."
         Audio: tactile click or surface sound on placement

10–18s   LORE
         Second sentence, withholds resolution:
         "The estate was sealed in 2003. We don't know
          who paid the storage fees until last year."
         Audio: ambient hum + voiceover

18–22s   SOFT CLOSE
         On-screen text: small monospace
         "LOT 0049-A / available now"
         Faint mechanical sound (winding, ticking)
         Slow fade to black
```

### The iron rule

**Never start the video with the full watch.** Always a fragment. The brain's pattern-completion drive is the algorithm hack.

The first 1.5 seconds is the most important real estate in your business. Steal these patterns.

3. Watch rotating slowly on a dark turntable, ambient hum

7. Watch placed on top of an open book, paper rustle
8. UV light passing over the dial, lume glowing
9. Watch being unwrapped from brown paper, no faces
10. Macro of the rotor spinning, frozen mid-rotation
11. Watch flipped over, caseback macro with fictional engraving visible
12. Light pulled across the dial in slow pan, indices catching
13. Strap being threaded through buckle, leather sound

## 5. Posting cadence

The non-negotiable schedule for the first 90 days.

```
MONDAY
  @brand        2 posts (1 Reveal + 1 Detail)
  @brand.notes  1 text post

TUESDAY
  @brand        2 posts (1 Reveal + 1 Frame)
  @brand.field  1 ambient post

WEDNESDAY
  @brand        2 posts (1 Reveal + 1 Found Document)
  @brand.notes  1 text post

THURSDAY
  @brand        2 posts (1 Reveal + 1 Detail)
  @brand.field  1 ambient post

FRIDAY
  @brand        2 posts (1 Reveal + 1 Frame)
  @brand.notes  1 text post

SATURDAY
  @brand        2 posts (best 2 from the week's overflow)
  @brand.notes  1 text post

SUNDAY
  @brand        2 posts (Detail + Frame, no reveal)
  @brand.field  1 longer ambient piece (60–90s)
```

Total per week:
- 14 main posts
- 4 field posts
- 5 notes posts
- **23 posts/week across the network**

### Post timing

Two windows daily for the main account:
- Morning slot: 8:00–9:30 AM local time
- Evening slot: 7:00–9:00 PM local time

Use Metricool, Later, or Buffer to schedule. **Never post manually.** Operator discipline = batched scheduling.

| Day | Account | Post type |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | @brand.field | Tease "new shipment received," 5s ambient |
| Day 2 | @brand | Reveal: first object of the estate |
| Day 3 | @brand | Reveal: second object + Detail of first |
| Day 4 | @brand | Reveal: third object + Frame |
| Day 5 | @brand | Reveal: fourth object + Found Document |
| Day 6 | @brand | Reveal: fifth object + Detail of third |
| Day 7 | @brand.notes | Closing line: "Estate 049 is closed." |

This creates **appointment viewing**. Returning viewers get prioritized by the algorithm. That's how you escape FYP roulette.

## 7. What NOT to do (anti-patterns)

These are TikTok behaviors that work for other niches but destroy this brand:

- ❌ Trending sounds (audio-mining the FYP)
- ❌ "Wait for it" hooks
- ❌ "POV: you walk into..."
- ❌ Day-in-the-life formats
- ❌ Face reveals
- ❌ "Here's why I started this brand" videos
- ❌ Behind-the-scenes "packing orders" videos (breaks fictional handler frame)
- ❌ Captions in TikTok's text generator (use your own monospace overlays)
- ❌ Hashtag spam (use 2–4 max: #watches #archive plus 1–2 niche)
- ❌ Replying to comments with brand voice broken
- ❌ Cross-posting unmodified to Instagram Reels (the aspect ratio + algorithm differs — modify)
- ❌ "What do you think?" engagement questions in captions
- ❌ Polls, "tag a friend who needs this," contest mechanics

## 8. Comment management

Comments are public-facing brand surface. They get replies in voice, or no reply.

### Reply rules

- Always in voice. Past tense, withholding, no exclamation, no emoji.
- Never break the world.
- If someone asks "how much?" — reply with the lot number and a link, no other context.
- If someone asks "where are you based?" — reply: *"The handling facility is in transit."* Never specify.
- If someone asks "is this real vintage?" — reply: *"Provenance is partial. Specifications are stated on each lot's record."* Never lie. Never confirm.
- Negative comments → ignore or one-line response in voice. Don't argue.

### Examples of in-voice replies

> Comment: "How can I get one?"
> Reply: *"LOT 0049-A. Available now."*

> Comment: "Is this a real Rolex?"
> Reply: *"No. The archive holds unbranded objects only."*

> Comment: "How much?"
> Reply: *"129. Lot record contains specifications."*

> Comment: "Where do you source these from?"
> Reply: *"Estates, brokerages, private handlers. Specific origins are documented per lot."*

## 11. The first viral video — how it usually happens

Almost every account in this niche has a first breakout video that 5x-es the follower count overnight. The pattern:

- It's almost never the one you expected
- It's almost always one of: a Detail post (extreme macro) or a Found Document post
- It typically hits 3–7 weeks in, not week 1
- It happens BECAUSE you posted the boring 60 videos before it that trained the algorithm on your visual fingerprint

**Don't optimize for the viral hit.** Optimize for the consistency that makes it possible.
