# 01 — BRAND BIBLE

This is the source-of-truth document. Everything else descends from here. Read it before every shoot, every email, every product launch.

---

## 1. The concept

> **An anonymous archive of unbranded mechanical objects, presented as recovered from estates that may or may not be real.**

The brand is not a watch store. It's an institution that handles objects. Watches are the most common artifact, but the brand could plausibly handle any small mechanical object with patina potential.

**Public framing:** *"Selected, not designed."*

**Internal truth:** A faceless ecom operation sourcing unbranded watches and objects from 1688/Yupoo and selling them under a tightly controlled aesthetic IP.

**The lie buyers tell themselves to justify purchasing:**
*"I'm not buying a cheap watch. I'm buying something most people don't know exists yet."*

That sentence is the entire psychological transaction. Every part of the system reinforces it.

---

## 2. The world rules

These are the laws of the fiction. Internal contradictions break the spell.

1. The brand never invented or designed anything. It only **selects** and **handles**.
2. Objects come from "estates" — fictional collections with numbers, locations, and sealed dates.
3. Locations are real cities, often in continental Europe, Eastern Europe, Japan, or specific industrial regions (Geneva, Antwerp, Milan, Copenhagen, Trieste, Osaka, Vilnius, Brno).
4. Dates are mostly **1970–2005**. Pre-digital, post-modern. No reference to anything after 2005 in mythology.
5. Provenance is always **partially unverifiable**. Never claim full authenticity. Imply, don't assert.
6. The brand is run by an unnamed "handler" or "section." Customers correspond with the institution, not a person.
7. Estates are sealed, opened, and closed. Objects are released, retained, withheld. Never "launched," "dropped," or "released."
8. There are no models, no influencers, no faces, no celebrities, no spokespersons. Ever.

---

## 3. The voice

The brand voice is **dry, archival, restrained**. Read like an estate handler writing a routine internal memo, not a marketer.

### Voice principles

- **Withhold.** Say less than the reader expects.
- **Specific over general.** *Geneva* not *Switzerland*. *A notebook* not *an item*. *1991* not *the early 90s*.
- **Past tense and passive voice.** *Was sealed in 2003. Has been preserved.*
- **No emotion.** Joy, excitement, urgency, gratitude — all absent.
- **No second person.** Never "you" or "your." Never address the reader.
- **No first person plural except in handler statements.** "We retained only the watch" is acceptable. "We're so excited to share" is not.

### Sentence rules

- Average sentence length: 8–12 words.
- Never two adjectives in a row.
- No exclamation points.
- No emojis. Anywhere. Including DMs and customer service.
- No questions to the reader.
- No rhetorical hooks ("Imagine if..." "What if...").

### Tone calibration examples

✅ *"Recovered from a closed brokerage in Antwerp. Found in a desk drawer alongside three unsent letters. We retained only the watch."*

✅ *"Origin unconfirmed. The seller claimed it had not been wound since 1998. The rotor moved on the second day."*

✅ *"Estate sealed in 2003 following a death without heirs. Items released to us in May. This is the last."*

❌ *"This stunning timepiece was crafted with attention to detail and is sure to elevate your wrist game!"*

❌ *"We're so excited to share this beautiful piece with our community 🔥"*

❌ *"Don't miss out — only 3 left in stock!"*

## 4. Forbidden words (locked, no exceptions)

These words break the world. They never appear in any brand surface — product pages, captions, emails, DMs, packaging.

```
luxury           premium          elegant          stunning
beautiful        gorgeous         crafted          exclusive
limited          drop             launch           release
sale             discount         deal             offer
amazing          incredible       perfect          must-have
you              your             yours            we're
guys             babe             fam              fire
flex             grail            hype             vibes
authentic        genuine          guaranteed       certified
```

**Forbidden punctuation:** `!` `?` (when addressing the reader), emojis of any kind, `...` for dramatic effect.

## 5. Naming shortlist

Pick one. Buy the .com, lock all 3 TikTok handles + IG, register Klaviyo and Shopify on the same name. **Do this within 24 hours of choosing.**

Criteria for the name:
- One word, 5–8 letters
- Not in English dictionary (or used unusually)
- No clear competitor on Google page 1
- Pronounceable but ambiguous origin
- No tribal/cringe associations

### Shortlist (test before committing)

**Group A — coded/institutional:**
- `Maison 042`
- `Section B`
- `Atelier 7`
- `Lot & Estate`

**Group B — single-word, ambiguous:**
- `Hörst`
- `Vellum`
- `Anker`
- `Forme`
- `Mörk`
- `Verge`
- `Soren`
- `Nocturne`
- `Solen`
- `Korbin`

**Group C — two-word, archival:**
- `Field & Estate`
- `North Vault`
- `Archive Section`
- `The Standing Order`

### Selection process

1. Pick top 3 from above (or invent your own using the same criteria).
2. Check `.com` availability. If `.com` is taken, kill it.
3. Check TikTok handle availability for the brand and `.field` and `.notes` variants.
4. Google the name. If anyone in fashion / horology / design uses it, kill it.
5. Say it out loud in a voiceover sample. If it sounds like a real estate handler announcing it on a phone, keep it.
6. Lock it. Buy everything immediately.

---

## 6. The single rule

> **Never break the world.**

No sales. No discount codes. No emojis. No "we" or "you." No CTAs. No face. No real-time trends. No replies in casual tone. No Black Friday, no Cyber Monday, no birthday emails, no abandoned cart "we missed you!" copy.

The discipline is the moat. Competitors can copy your videos. They cannot copy 12 months of consistent voice.
